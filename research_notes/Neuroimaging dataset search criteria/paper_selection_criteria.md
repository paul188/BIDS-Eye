# How published neuroimaging studies describe searching for and selecting open/public datasets

Method note (applies to all sections). Sources were gathered in three ways:
1. Web search and fetch of known papers: Markiewicz 2021 (OpenNeuro), MOABB 2024, EEGDash Corpus 2026, Thomas et al. 2022 and REVE 2025.
2. A scripted Europe PMC full-text harvest on 2026-11-14. It ran 20 phrase queries such as `"OpenNeuro" AND "datasets were selected"`, `"OpenNeuro" AND "inclusion criteria" AND "datasets"`, `"OpenNeuro" AND "brain age"`, `"OpenNeuro" AND "MEG" AND "datasets" AND "selected"` and `"OpenfMRI" AND "we selected"`. Queries were restricted to open-access papers from 2017 to 2026. This gave 341 candidate papers. The script then pulled their full-text XML and kept sentences that contained selection words (criteria, selected, excluded, searched, at least, required) next to a repository word (OpenNeuro, OpenfMRI, NITRC, INDI, NeuroVault, publicly available). 150 papers had such sentences, and I read all of them by hand.
3. Paragraph-level extraction from the most relevant hits, so the criteria could be quoted in context.

Most of the 150 hits turned out to be one of two kinds. Some are data descriptors that deposit data on OpenNeuro. Others reuse a single named dataset (for example "OpenNeuro ds004504") and state no dataset-selection criteria. Only a minority describe choosing among many public datasets. The tally below uses the **14 papers whose dataset-level selection criteria I could verify verbatim**. It is a small, keyword-biased convenience sample, not a systematic review.

## Q1. What inclusion/exclusion criteria do papers that pool or reuse public datasets state?

### Takeaway
Pooling and benchmark papers almost always filter by **modality / required scan types** and usually by **task or paradigm**. Next come **access/licence**, **data quality/integrity**, **acquisition parameters** (TR, voxel size, field strength, sequence, sampling rate) and **minimum sample size**. Each of these appears in roughly a third of the sample. Diagnosis/health status and age appear in fewer than a third. Sex, species, site/country, longitudinal design and derivative availability almost never appear as dataset-level filters.

### Cited Findings

**MRI preprocessing / segmentation tool validation (pooling many OpenNeuro datasets)**
- fMRIPrep validation (Esteban et al., 2019, *Nature Methods*): "Participants were drawn from a multiplicity of studies available in OpenfMRI, accessed on September 30, 2017. Studies were sampled uniformly (four participants each)… only datasets including at least one T1-weighted (T1w) and one BOLD per subject run were included. Datasets containing BIDS errors (DS000210), and degenerate data (many T1w images of DS000223 are skull-stripped) at the time of access were discarded. Similarly, very-narrow FoV BOLD datasets (DS000172, DS000217, and DS000232) were also excluded. In total, 54 datasets (46 single-session datasets, 8 multi-session) were included." The paper also describes the resulting vendor mix ("∼70% SIEMENS, ∼14% PHILIPS, ∼14% GE") and "1.5T and 3T systems". It tabulates sessions, tasks, runs, TR, #TR and voxel size per dataset. Criteria used: required modalities, BIDS validity, data integrity, field of view, access date. — [Esteban et al. 2019, PMC6319393](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6319393) (doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0235-4)
- deepmriprep (Fisch et al., 2026, *Nature Computational Science*): "Out of the over 700 datasets available at OpenNeuro at the time of compilation (10 November 2021), each dataset that contained at least five T1w MRIs from at least five adult HCs was included, resulting in 208 datasets… All OpenNeuro datasets that contained fewer than ten adult HCs after this quality control were excluded." The test set was "OpenNeuro-Kids": "each dataset that contained at least 5 T1w MRIs from at least 5 HCs in the age range from 2 to 12 years was included, resulting in 18 datasets." Criteria used: modality, health status, age band, minimum N, visual QC, snapshot date. — [Fisch et al. 2026, doi:10.1038/s43588-026-00953-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-026-00953-7) (Europe PMC PMC13021509)
- Head-motion estimation in structural MRI (Bricout et al., 2026, *Front. Neurosci.*): "we use OpenNeuro to find other suitable datasets to test our models. Our criteria are: Includes at least one subject between 5 and 21. Field strength should be three Tesla. Acquisition protocol should be similar to MPRAGE. No prospective motion correction techniques (e.g., vNavs)." The result was 847 volumes from 11 studies. The authors were "especially interested in ds000144 and ds003568 as they do not use Siemens hardware." — [Bricout et al. 2026, doi:10.3389/fnins.2026.1817743](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1817743)
- FastSurfer (Henschel et al., 2020, *NeuroImage*): "MRI volumes from eight publicly available datasets were selected for training, testing, and for extensive validation". The harvested text gives no explicit criteria. — [Henschel et al. 2020, doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117012)
- BrainScape (Yasinzai et al., 2025, *Imaging Neuroscience*): "a curated collection of 160 publicly available MRI datasets". Exclusions are subject-level QC: "excluding MRIs with artifacts, corrupted and poor-quality scans" and incompletely skull-stripped scans. Exclusions are "documented in dedicated dataset-specific configuration files". — [Yasinzai et al. 2025, doi:10.1162/imag.a.944](https://doi.org/10.1162/imag.a.944)

**fMRI QC / reproducibility / multi-pipeline studies**
- FMRI QC Research Topic (Taylor et al., 2023, *Front. Neurosci.*): "we browsed available data repositories that had open use agreements, including ABIDE-1 and ABIDE-2, AOMIC, FCP, MPI-LEMON, SALD, and SLIM, as well as a large number of OpenNeuro collections. In total, over 230 separate resting state data collections were initially examined… The first selection stage was to find collections with the following properties: Having >12 subjects, each of whom has at least one EPI and one T1w volume in the same session directory. EPI: TR > 1.5 s, all voxel edges < 4.1 mm, number of volumes > 100, non-zero srow values in the NIFTI header. T1w: all voxel edges < 2.1 mm, non-zero srow values in the NIFTI header." The editorial states explicitly that diagnosis was **not** a filter: "Some repositories originally contained explicit categorization of subjects as 'control' and non-control… those designations did not influence data selection". — [Taylor et al. 2023, doi:10.3389/fnins.2023.1205928](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2023.1205928)
- Software-variability reanalysis (Bowring, Maumet & Nichols, 2019, *Hum. Brain Mapp.*): "These datasets were chosen following an extensive selection procedure (carried out between May 2016 and November 2016), whereby we vetted the associated publication for each dataset stored in the repository." The studies "were selected on the basis that they reported clearly defined regions of brain activation and utilised analysis procedures feasible across the three software packages." In the end, "the three studies analysed here were found to be the only datasets hosted on OpenfMRI suited to the aims of our investigation." The 2022 follow-up repeats this ("the need for task-based fMRI data with analysis methods compatible within AFNI, FSL, and SPM"). — [Bowring et al. 2019, doi:10.1002/hbm.24603](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24603); [Bowring et al. 2022, doi:10.1002/hbm.25713](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25713)
- Memory-consolidation reanalysis (Bartzioka & Khosravi, 2026, *NeuroImage: Reports*): "All neuroimaging datasets analyzed in this study were obtained from publicly available repositories adhering to the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) standard and were downloaded from OpenNeuro… Datasets were selected based on their alignment with the consolidation clusters described above, together with task metadata completeness, preprocessing compatibility, and sufficient sample size." — [Bartzioka & Khosravi 2026, doi:10.1016/j.ynirp.2026.100398](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ynirp.2026.100398)

**Searching OpenNeuro by keyword (closest to "we searched OpenNeuro for…")**
- NEBULA101 data descriptor (Rampinini et al., 2025, *Scientific Data*) is the most explicit OpenNeuro search query found: "a search on http://openneuro.org for BIDS-valid MRI raw/derivative datasets with more than 50 healthy adults, including the keyword 'language' and accompanied by a published or pre-registered data descriptor, yields the following entries (Table 1), with two of the datasets (ds004215, ds000243) being listed but having no relationship with language (and thus not being included in the below table)." Criteria used: BIDS validity, modality, raw vs derivative, N > 50, health status, adult age, topic keyword, published descriptor. Keyword matching gave false positives that had to be removed by hand. — [Rampinini et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41597-024-04357-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04357-y)
- HED LANG (Denissen et al., 2024, *Scientific Data*): "To identify sample datasets to annotate, we searched for suitable BIDS datasets available in OpenNeuro. We selected fMRI datasets for experiments presenting isolated words." — [Denissen et al. 2024, doi:10.1038/s41597-024-04282-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04282-0)
- Survey of psychiatric/neurological MRI databases (Tanaka et al., 2024, *Psychiatry Clin. Neurosci.*): "Step 1: Search the datasets for each disease in the following order: Search for 'disease name + MRI' in Scientific Data; Search by 'disease name + MRI' in data repositories such as OpenNeuro and Synapse; Search by 'disease name + MRI -neuroVault -figshare -mendeley -integbio' using a Google dataset search; Search PubMed…". Example: "Keywords used for the search were eating disorders, anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder, and MRI." For each hit they recorded "Data access", "Number of samples" (per diagnostic group, e.g. "HC: 21, Mild MDD: 51"), "Contents" (T1w, rs-fMRI, task-fMRI, DWI, clinical information) and "Number of citations". — [Tanaka et al. 2024, doi:10.1111/pcn.13717](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcn.13717)
- Stress/physiological-signal dataset review (Chatzaki & Tsiknakis, 2025, *Sensors*): "For the search on OpenNeuro, the modality of EEG was chosen and combined with the keywords describing the affective condition." Datasets were excluded over unclear availability. For example, SWEET "was excluded due to doubts on the terms and conditions of availability". — [Chatzaki & Tsiknakis 2025, doi:10.3390/s25237108](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25237108)
- Normative open-access database review (Isherwood et al., 2021, *PLoS One*) used three criteria. Databases "had to be normative, that is, made up of individuals that were reported as healthy at the time of scanning". They had to be "a collection of curated images… acquired to be of similar composition (based on sequences and/or sites)". And they had to be "open-access… without access barriers. Such access barriers include, for example, memberships, a specific institutional position… or the requirement of some type of institutional infrastructure (e.g., Federalwide Assurance)." "41 databases were initially identified. After the first screening, 5 were excluded on the basis of access requirements." — [Isherwood et al. 2021, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0248341](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0248341)

**EEG/BCI benchmarks and foundation-model corpora**
- MOABB benchmark (Chevallier et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.15319): "we chose to restrict to three common BCI paradigms, namely MI, ERP, and SSVEP". Datasets were excluded "as there are exotic or unreadable data formats, BCI protocols that are used for a unique dataset, and unavailable code". The paper evaluates on 36 public datasets and tabulates subjects, channels, classes, trials per class/session/run, trial length, sampling frequency, sessions and runs. — [Chevallier et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15319v1)
- EEGDash Corpus (Shahi, Aristimunha & Delorme, 2026, arXiv:2611.06356) has 77 datasets (56 OpenNeuro, 21 NEMAR). Exclusions: "Four are clinical archives that require registration or a data use agreement and forbid redistribution"; "Eight are small brain–computer-interface sets under no-derivatives licences, which forbid a converted copy"; one dataset "is sampled at 100 Hz". Per recording: "Recordings of 10 seconds or less, or sampled below 200 Hz, are skipped. Channels typed as EEG are kept… and channels without a position are dropped." Benchmark source datasets "are refused by identifier". — [Shahi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06356v1)
- REVE EEG foundation model (2025) pretrains on 92 sources (OpenNeuro, MOABB, TUH): 24,274 subjects and 61,415 hours. A search-result summary says the authors name more selective curation (removing low-quality recordings, balancing distributions) as future work. I did not verify this in the full text. — [REVE, arXiv:2510.21585](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21585)
- A unified EEG foundation-model registry (Shi et al., 2026, *Brain-X*) reportedly screened public EEG datasets updated 2020–2026, starting with the requirement that a dataset contain EEG signal. OpenNeuro contributes 173 datasets. This comes from a search snippet only; the publisher page returned 403. — [Shi et al. 2026, doi:10.1002/brx2.70046](https://doi.org/10.1002/brx2.70046)

**Statistic-map reuse (NeuroVault)**
- Germani, Fromont & Maumet (2023, *GigaScience*) filtered NeuroVault through its API on metadata that is "mandatory and specified for all maps such as the modality (e.g., 'fMRI-BOLD'…), the type of statistic (e.g., 'T map' or 'Z map'), or the cognitive paradigm". One collection "was excluded… due to missing metadata (i.e., 'is_valid' is false)". The result was 28,532 maps. They note that paradigm labels were "not labeled in a standardized way". — [Germani et al. 2023, doi:10.1093/gigascience/giad029](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giad029)

**Deep-learning pretraining on OpenNeuro fMRI**
- Thomas, Ré & Poldrack (2022, NeurIPS) pretrain on "11,980 experimental runs of 1,726 individuals across 34 datasets", all "publicly available (under a Creative Commons CC0 license) through OpenNeuro.org". In the fetched text, the only criterion I could verify was the licence/source; no other selection criteria were found. — [Thomas et al. 2022, arXiv:2206.11417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11417)

**Subject-level criteria applied after a dataset is chosen** (common in single-dataset reuses)
- "After excluding 18 participants without the required baseline age or sex information" (trigeminal neuralgia, OpenNeuro ds005713). — [Li et al. 2026, doi:10.3389/fneur.2026.1964745](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1964745)
- The cohort was defined from "BDI measurements provided in the participant-level clinical metadata". — [Ramaiah et al. 2026, Int J Methods Psychiatr Res](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13659267)
- Normative MEG model: "All participants represented independent observations; no repeated measures were included." — [Zamanzadeh et al. 2026, doi:10.1038/s42003-026-09825-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-09825-2)
- "Only parcellations based on T1-weighted images that had passed quality control procedures implemented by the original data sources were included." — [Ge et al. 2026, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2521055123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2521055123)

### Inferences
- The criteria fall into three layers:
  - **Layer 1, findability:** modality, task/topic keyword, diagnosis/health, age.
  - **Layer 2, technical suitability:** required scan pairs in the same session, TR, voxel size, field strength, sequence family, number of volumes, sampling rate, recording length, channel type and positions, BIDS validity, header sanity, field of view.
  - **Layer 3, legal/provenance:** licence, DUA, redistribution, a linked publication or data descriptor.
  
  A search engine that only handles Layer 1 will under-serve methods and ML users, whose criteria are mostly Layers 2–3.
- Numeric thresholds are very common: ">12 subjects", "at least five T1w MRIs from at least five adult HCs", "more than 50 healthy adults", "TR > 1.5 s", "voxel edges < 4.1 mm", "< 200 Hz", "between 5 and 21". So range and comparison operators on participant counts, ages and acquisition parameters matter.
- "Same session" co-occurrence constraints (EPI and T1w in the same session; T1w and BOLD per subject) are a distinctive pattern that a flat modality filter cannot express.
- Subject-level availability of phenotype columns (age, sex, BDI) decides post-selection exclusions. That makes "does participants.tsv have column X" a useful query.

### Gaps
- I could not access full methods for several high-value pooling papers. These include Leonardsen et al. 2022 brain age (21 datasets, N=53,542; medRxiv returned 403), Bethlehem et al. 2022 brain charts, the full REVE methods and the Brain-X EEG registry (403). Their criteria are not in the tally.
- I found no paper using the exact phrase "we searched OpenNeuro". Phrasings in use are "we use OpenNeuro to find other suitable datasets", "a search on http://openneuro.org for…", "we searched for suitable BIDS datasets available in OpenNeuro", and "For the search on OpenNeuro, the modality of EEG was chosen and combined with the keywords…".
- Animal imaging: no animal-data pooling paper with explicit dataset-selection criteria came up. Species was never stated as a filter, only implied as human. Markiewicz et al. note OpenNeuro holds mouse, rat, NHP, dog and pig datasets.
- iEEG, MEG and PET pooling papers with explicit dataset-level criteria were not found in this harvest. The iEEG hits were single-dataset reuses (e.g. ds004100).

## Q2. How often does each criterion appear? (tally)

### Takeaway
In a verified sample of 14 papers that describe selecting among multiple public datasets or databases, the most frequent criteria are modality or required scan types (about 12/14) and task/paradigm/topic (8/14). Next are data quality/integrity (6/14), access/licence (5/14) and acquisition parameters (5/14). Then come minimum sample size, health/diagnosis, BIDS compliance and snapshot date (4/14 each), and age (3/14). Sex, species, site/country, longitudinal design, scanner vendor as a filter, and phenotype availability as a dataset-level filter each appeared in 0/14.

### Cited Findings
Sample (n = 14): fMRIPrep [Esteban 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-018-0235-4); deepmriprep [Fisch 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-026-00953-7); [Bricout 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1817743); FMRI QC [Taylor 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2023.1205928); NEBULA101 [Rampinini 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04357-y); HED LANG [Denissen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04282-0); [Tanaka 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcn.13717); [Chatzaki 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25237108); [Bowring 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24603); [Bartzioka 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ynirp.2026.100398); [Isherwood 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0248341); MOABB [Chevallier 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15319v1); EEGDash [Shahi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06356v1); NeuroVault [Germani 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giad029).

- Context on reuse: Markiewicz et al. "identified 165 publications that reused OpenNeuro datasets… A total of 111 OpenNeuro datasets were reused at least once… 31 of the 165 papers reused at least two datasets, with a maximum of 40 datasets reused (Esteban et al., 2019)". Reuses were found by "searching Google Scholar for the term 'OpenNeuro'", which the authors call an underestimate. — [Markiewicz et al. 2021, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.71774) (text via [bioRxiv v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.06.28.450168v3.full) and Europe PMC PMC8550750)

### Inferences
- In this sample about 1 in 5 reuse papers pools multiple datasets, which matches Markiewicz's 31/165. Most OpenNeuro reuse is single-dataset work that names an accession number. The multi-dataset "search and select" use case is a minority of reuses, but it is where explicit multi-criteria filtering happens.
- Criteria differ by user type:
  - Tool and ML builders (Esteban, Fisch, Bricout, Taylor, Shahi, Chevallier) stress technical and licence criteria.
  - Topic-driven scientists (Rampinini, Denissen, Bartzioka, Tanaka, Chatzaki) stress task/topic, diagnosis and population.
- Low counts for sex, site and longitudinal design may come from sample bias. Brain-age and normative-modelling papers, whose full methods I could not read, probably use age and health status more often.

### Gaps
- n = 14 is small and the counts are approximate (for example, "modality" was sometimes implicit). A systematic review over Markiewicz's 165 reuse papers, or a newer citation list, would be needed for robust frequencies.
- Europe PMC open-access full text under-represents arXiv/NeurIPS ML papers. That probably understates licence, sampling-rate and file-format criteria.

## Q3. Which criteria are hard to answer from metadata (need README, paper, or data inspection)?

### Takeaway
Many stated criteria cannot be answered from OpenNeuro's indexed metadata:
- Semantic task/topic fit ("alignment with the consolidation clusters", "experiments presenting isolated words", "clearly defined regions of brain activation") needs the README, events files or the original paper.
- Health status, age and minimum counts per group need participants.tsv parsing.
- Acquisition details (field strength, MPRAGE-like sequence, prospective motion correction, FoV) need JSON sidecars or data inspection.
- Quality and integrity (skull-stripped images, header srow, corrupted files) need the data itself.
- Licence nuance beyond CC0, and whether a published data descriptor or paper exists, need external lookups.

### Cited Findings
- Keyword search gives false positives that need manual reading. Rampinini et al.'s OpenNeuro search for 'language' returned "two of the datasets (ds004215, ds000243) being listed but having no relationship with language". — [Rampinini et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04357-y)
- Selection needed reading the associated papers: "we vetted the associated publication for each dataset stored in the repository", which took six months (May–November 2016). — [Bowring et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24603)
- Data-content problems only visible by inspecting the files: "degenerate data (many T1w images of DS000223 are skull-stripped)", "BIDS errors", "very-narrow FoV BOLD datasets". MRIQC was also used "to select the set of test images". — [Esteban et al. 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6319393)
- Header-level checks: "non-zero srow values in the NIFTI header"; per-subject "at least one EPI and one T1w volume in the same session directory". — [Taylor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2023.1205928)
- Acquisition specifics not exposed as search facets: "Field strength should be three Tesla. Acquisition protocol should be similar to MPRAGE. No prospective motion correction techniques (e.g., vNavs)." — [Bricout et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1817743)
- Missing documentation in OpenNeuro entries: "the precise sampling frequency and preprocessing steps… are not explicitly documented in the OpenNeuro entry" (about ds004504). — [Esteve et al. 2025, Sci Rep, doi:10.1038/s41598-025-23686-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-23686-5)
- Release notes and events files decide which participants can be used: "seven had missing or corrupted data files… as documented in the dataset release notes", and "one (sub-38) was excluded because its behavioural log contained no perception or imagination event codes". — [Gao & Diniz 2026, Brain Topogr, doi:10.1007/s10548-026-01238-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10548-026-01238-y)
- Licence nuance: "no-derivatives licences, which forbid a converted copy"; DUA-gated archives "forbid redistribution". — [Shahi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06356v1). Ambiguous availability terms also led to exclusion — [Chatzaki & Tsiknakis 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25237108)
- Non-standard labels in metadata: NeuroVault paradigm fields were "not labeled in a standardized way (i.e., use of terms that are specific for a study instead of generic terms…)". — [Germani et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giad029)
- The repository's own view: "At present, a limited amount of dataset-level metadata is collected beyond that present within the BIDS metadata". Ontology linking "will provide the basis for more powerful queries of the archive." To characterize content, the OpenNeuro team "searched the text associated with OpenNeuro datasets to identify terms related to psychological concepts and tasks as defined in the Cognitive Atlas ontology". — [Markiewicz et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.06.28.450168v3.full)
- OpenNeuro's search UI exposes facets such as modality, species, age, number of participants, diagnosis and task. I saw this in a search-result summary of openneuro.org/search and did not verify it on the live page. — [OpenNeuro search](https://openneuro.org/search)
- Diagnosis labels may be in participants files but are sometimes deliberately ignored: "those designations did not influence data selection". — [Taylor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2023.1205928)

### Inferences
Possible design implications for a natural-language OpenNeuro/BIDS search engine:
- **Index derived facets** that users filter on numerically: per-dataset counts of healthy controls vs patients, age range from participants.tsv, sex counts, and number of subjects with *both* modality X and Y in the same session.
- **Index acquisition facets from JSON sidecars:** MagneticFieldStrength, RepetitionTime, voxel size, sequence name/type (MPRAGE), number of volumes, EEG SamplingFrequency, channel counts and recording duration.
- **Index task semantics** from task names, events.tsv/HED and README, mapped to an ontology (Cognitive Atlas) to cut keyword false positives like Rampinini's "language" hits.
- **Expose licence and redistribution status**, and whether a linked paper or data descriptor exists (DOI in dataset_description.json `ReferencesAndLinks`).
- **Expose quality/integrity signals** where cheap: BIDS validator errors/warnings, skull-stripped/defaced flags, MRIQC metrics if derivatives exist.
- **Support versioned snapshots:** papers record "accessed on" dates, so returning dataset version tags helps reproducibility.

### Gaps
- I did not systematically check which of these fields OpenNeuro's current GraphQL search actually indexes. The facet list above is from a search summary, not verified.
- No quantitative estimate was found in the literature of how much time researchers spend reading READMEs or papers to decide on datasets. Bowring's six-month vetting is anecdotal.
